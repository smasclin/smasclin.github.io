Course averages pool all sections in the window (respondent-weighted). 14 common survey items, each rated 1 (strongly disagree) – 7 (strongly agree).

1 — The six courses at a glance

Universal strength. “Treated with respect” is the highest-scoring item in every one of the six courses (6.06–6.90). Students feel safe and respected regardless of course level or size.
Shared growth area. “Feedback on my work was valuable” is the lowest-scoring item in four of the six courses and, on average, the joint-lowest item overall. The most consistent, addressable gap.
The size effect. Smaller upper-division courses (ESS 110, 190, 015) sit ~0.4–1.1 points above the large intro GE courses (ESS 001, 002, 050) on the global index — a structural pattern, not a teaching-quality one.

3 — The class-size and delivery effect

Class size vs score — every section, coloured by delivery
Each dot is one section (Spring-2025 / Spring-2026 ESS 002 combine their two lectures). Larger sections sit lower; smaller online and seminar sections sit higher. Size and delivery are entangled here — see the caveat below.
Within-course delivery: regular term vs summer online
The cleanest delivery comparison holds the course constant. For all three courses taught in both modes, the summer online run scores higher — but those cohorts are far smaller and self-selected.
Honest caveat for a review
Delivery mode and class structure are confounded in this portfolio: hybrid means ESS 001 only (the large intro GE that scores lowest for size reasons), seminar means ESS 190 only, and online means small self-selected summer cohorts. The defensible claim is not “online teaching is better,” but rather: smaller and self-selected sections rate higher, and the same instructor delivers consistently across every format. The within-course panel on the right is the most rigorous slice because it removes the course confound.
